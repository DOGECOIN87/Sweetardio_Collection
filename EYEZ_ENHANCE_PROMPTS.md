# Eye Trait — AI Enhancement Prompts

Prompts for re-rendering the eleven **Eyez** assets so they read as dimensional
and glossy instead of flat vector fills — while staying the cartoon eyes the
collection is built on.

Companion sheet: `catalog/eyez_reference_sheet.png` (all eleven at 1:1).
Companion docs: `SKIN_ENHANCE_PROMPTS.md`, `MOUTHZ_ENHANCE_PROMPTS.md`.

---

## 1. Read this before generating anything

**The target is a dimensional cartoon eye, not a photoreal one.** The whole
look of this collection is a flat graphic face sitting on a photoreal body —
that contrast *is* the style. An eye rendered as a realistic human eyeball
destroys the character and stops matching the other 4,443 tokens. What these
prompts ask for is the treatment in the reference art: keep the exact graphic
shape and colour, add gloss, gradient, a real catchlight, and a sense of a wet
curved surface under the outline.

**Three hard constraints, all of them mechanical:**

1. **Width is load-bearing.** `ball_fit()` in `generator.py` sizes every skin
   ball from the widest eye:
   `factor = eye_width / (0.92 × ball_width)`. The widest eye today is Cyan at
   288px, forcing a 1.152× ball upscale. An eye that comes back wider inflates
   the face on every token that uses it. `register_trait.py` fits the art back
   into the original footprint, so it cannot happen by accident — but generate
   at the right proportion anyway, because the alternative is the art getting
   squeezed to fit.
2. **The black keyline is not decoration.** These eyes composite over five very
   different skins — near-white Alien, dark chocolate Black, mid-tan White,
   saturated Cyan gel, bright Gold Foil. The heavy dark outline is the only
   reason they stay readable on all five. Thin it or soften it into a
   render-style edge and the eyes disappear on the dark skins.
3. **Position is fixed.** Eyes are pasted at exact canvas coordinates and the
   skin ball is sized around them. Nothing moves to meet a drifted layer.

**Colour changes have a downstream cost.** `traits/eyez_compat.json` holds a
per-background blocklist *and* soft weights, generated by
`asset_assessment/build_eyez_compat.py` from each eye's measured dominant
colour — it is what keeps blue eyes off blue backgrounds. Six eyes carry
entries: Blue, Cerise, Cyan, Beady, Cyborg, Smug. If an enhanced eye shifts
hue, regenerate that file:

```bash
python3 asset_assessment/build_eyez_compat.py
```

## 3. How to run these

Eleven assets share one set of rules and differ only in subject, so this doc is
a **master prompt with a slot**, not eleven near-identical walls of text. Paste
§4, then paste the one asset block from §5 where it says `>>> ASSET <<<`. To
hand off everything at once instead, use §6 with the reference sheet.

Open a fresh chat per eye, attach only that eye's PNG, and check the result
against §7 before keeping it.

## 4. Master prompt

```
Re-render the attached image as a higher-quality version of the SAME artwork.
It is a single trait layer from a collectible series: a pair of cartoon eyes
that gets composited onto a character's face.

THIS IS NOT A REALISM CONVERSION. The style is a flat graphic cartoon face on
a photorealistic body, and that contrast is the whole look. Keep it a cartoon
eye. What I want added is dimension WITHIN that cartoon: a sense of a smooth
wet curved surface, a soft gradient instead of a dead flat fill, a real
catchlight, and clean soft shading where forms overlap.

KEEP EXACTLY AS THEY ARE:
- The outline shape and silhouette of both eyes, and the gap between them.
- The overall width and height proportion. Do not make the eyes rounder,
  larger, wider apart, or more symmetrical than they already are.
- The heavy black keyline. It must stay solid, opaque and the same weight —
  this artwork sits on backgrounds ranging from near-white to near-black, and
  that outline is the only thing keeping it readable. Do not thin it, blur it,
  soften it into a shadow, or replace it with a rendered edge.
- The colour of every filled area.
- Both eyes stay a matched pair in the same relative positions.

ADD:
- A smooth gradient across each filled area so it reads as a curved surface
  rather than a flat sticker.
- One clean, bright catchlight per eye, upper left, plus a smaller dimmer
  secondary bounce on the lower right. Crisp-edged like a cartoon highlight,
  not a soft photographic bloom.
- A subtle darker gradient where the eye meets its outline, so the surface
  looks like it curves away under the line.
- A wet, glossy finish overall.

DO NOT:
- Do not make it a realistic human eye. No skin, no eyelids, no eyelashes, no
  eyebrows, no tear ducts, no veins, no iris fibre detail.
- Do not add a face, a head, a body, or any surrounding character.
- Do not add a background, a surface, a scene, or a cast shadow.
- Do not add glow, bloom, or any halo outside the artwork's outline.
- Do not add text, logos, watermarks, or sparkles.
- Do not change the number of eyes or add extra elements.

OUTPUT: the eyes alone, on a fully transparent background, square canvas,
highest resolution available. Crisp anti-aliased edge with no colour fringe.
If you cannot output true transparency, render on a flat, uniform pure green
(#00FF00) field with no shadow, gradient, or spill onto the green.

>>> ASSET <<<
```

## 7. Acceptance checklist

- [ ] **Still a cartoon.** No eyelids, lashes, brows, skin, or realistic iris
      detail. It should look like it belongs on the same character as before.
- [ ] **Keyline intact.** The black outline is as heavy and as opaque as the
      original. Check it against the dark skins, not just on white.
- [ ] **Same silhouette and width.** Overlay on the original at 50% opacity —
      outer edges track, and the pair sits the same distance apart.
- [ ] **Same hue.** Especially Blue, Cerise, Cyan, Beady, Cyborg and Smug,
      which carry `eyez_compat.json` entries keyed to their measured colour.
- [ ] **Expression unchanged.** Squints still squint, side-eye still looks
      sideways, smug eyes are still closed.
- [ ] **Clean edge.** No baked shadow, outer glow, or backdrop colour surviving
      in the anti-aliased pixels — any of those show as a ring on the skin ball.
- [ ] **Reads at thumbnail size.** Zoom to 10%: it must still parse instantly.

## 8. Retry lines

- **Went photoreal:** `Too realistic. Go back to a flat cartoon eye — no
  eyelids, lashes, skin, or iris detail. Keep the graphic shapes and the heavy
  black outline; add only gloss, a gradient, and a catchlight.`
- **Outline weakened:** `Restore the heavy black outline at its original
  weight and full opacity. It must stay a solid graphic keyline, not a soft
  rendered edge.`
- **Shape drifted:** `The outline must match the attached image exactly — same
  width, same height, same spacing between the two eyes.`
- **Expression changed:** `Keep the original expression exactly: <squinted /
  looking to the side / closed>. Do not reposition or resize the pupils.`
- **Glow escaped:** `Remove any glow or halo outside the artwork's outline.
  Light may fall inside the shape only.`

## 9. Getting the result back in

```bash
python3 asset_assessment/register_trait.py enhanced.png "Blue.png" \
    --preview /tmp/blue_ab.png
```

The class is inferred from the filename. It keys out the backdrop, despills,
crops, and pastes the art onto a 1393×1393 canvas at the original footprint and
position, then reports the worst-case skin-ball upscale that eye forces — the
`ball_fit` coupling from §1 — so a proportion change shows up as a number.
Add `--replace` to write into `traits/eyez/`.

Then: **keep the filename identical** (`eyez_compat.json` is keyed by it),
regenerate the compat file if any hue moved, and verify in a real render:

```bash
python3 asset_assessment/build_eyez_compat.py        # only if colours changed
python3 asset_assessment/render_sample_sheet.py --n 25 --cols 5 --cell 500 \
    --out /tmp/eye_check.png
```

Check the enhanced eyes against a **dark** skin and a **pale** skin in the same
sheet — that is where a weakened keyline shows up first.
